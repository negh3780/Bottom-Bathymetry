\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}


We are interested in the estimation of the Bathymetry of shallow water flow from error corrupted surface wave height measurements. 
The equations of motion are as follows:
\begin{align}
    &h_t + (hu)_x + (hv)_y = 0,
    \label{eq:sw:h}\\
   &(hu)_t + \bigl(hu^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}gh^2\bigr)_{\!x} + (huv)_y = -ghb_x,
    \label{eq:sw:u}\\
   &(hv)_t + (huv)_x + \bigl(hv^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}gh^2\bigr)_{\!y} = -ghb_y,
   \label{eq:sw:v}
\end{align}
where $h$ is the water column height, $(u,v)$ is the horizontal velocity and $g$ is the 
gravitational constant.  We supplement \eqref{eq:sw:h}--\eqref{eq:sw:v} with
initial conditions 
\begin{align}
	h(0,x,y) = h_0(x,y), \quad u(0,x,y) = u_0(x,y), \quad v(0,x,y) = v_0(x,y),
\end{align}
and boundary conditions.  Regarding the spatial domain $D$ on which \eqref{eq:sw:h}--\eqref{eq:sw:v}  and associated boundary 
conditions a pragmatic starting point would be to consider a periodic box of length $[-L,L]$ for some $L \gg 1$.\footnote{In any case the fluid domain $D$ of definition needs to be defined and in general $D$ is not fixed. Even if
we imposed e.g. periodic boundary conditions the fluid motion here is hyperbolic and can form
shocks in finite time.   At least from an analysis point of view we may wish to 
impose further conditions (small data? viscous dissipation?) to obtain a more mathematically (and numerically?) tractable set 
of equations.}   

We will measure the surface elevation $\eta$ that is
\begin{align}
	\eta(t,x,y) :=  h(x,y,t)+b(x,y),
\end{align}
sparsely and with corrupting noise in our observations.  More specifically our model for a set of wave height observations
$\bfY= (y_1, …, y_n)$ are 
\begin{align}
\label{eq:obs:proceedure}
y_i = \eta(t_i, x_i, y_i; b) + \epsilon_i
\end{align}
where $((t_1, x_1, y_1),\ldots, (t_n, x_n, y_n))$ are the observation locations and $\beps = (\epsilon_1, \ldots, \epsilon_n)$ is an (additive) error or noise
corruption in the measurement of $(\eta(t_1, x_1, y_n; b), \ldots,  \eta(t_n, x_n, y_n; b))$.\footnote{More generally we can just as well consider $y_i = \Obs_j(\eta(b)) + \epsilon_i$ where the $\Obs_j$'s are observation
operators.  These $\Obs_j$ could be spatial temporal averages of $\eta$.  Of course the special case of spatial-temporal point observations is given by $\Obs_j(\eta) = \eta(t_j, x_j, y_j)$.}

Suppose that the $\beps \sim N(0, \Sigma)$, that is a Gaussian mean free error, with Covariance $\Sigma$.  Note that this $\Sigma$ includes the possibility of correlations in the observation noise.  
In this situation we then have a  likelihood of $b$ of the form
\begin{align}
	\mathcal{L}(b; \bfY) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{ (2\pi)^n \det(\Sigma)}} \exp\left( -\frac{1}{2} | \Sigma^{1/2} (\bfY - H(b)) |^2\right).
\end{align}
Thus, in statistical language, optimizing the quadratic loss function
\begin{align}
 \ell(b, \bfY) = | \Sigma^{1/2} (\bfY - H(b)) |^2
\end{align}
amounts to computing the Maximum Likelihood estimator for the measurement model \eqref{eq:obs:proceedure}.


Here we would like to treat the problem of formulating, resolving and analyzin the Bayesian posterior 
for the bottom boundary given data points $\bfY= (y_1, …, y_n)$ as 
\begin{align}
	\label{eq:bayes:bath}
\mu(d b) \propto \exp\left( -\frac{1}{2} | \Sigma^{1/2} (\bfY - H(b)) |^2\right) \mu_0(db),
\end{align}
where $H$ is the `forward-map' defined as
\begin{align}
	\label{eq:forward:map}
	H(b) = (\eta(t_1, x_1, y_n; b), \ldots,  \eta(t_n, x_n, y_n; b)),
\end{align}
where $\mu_0$ is the prior (probability) distribution on the unknown Bathymetry $b$.
Thus, in order to specify the estimation and uncertainty quantification problem around \eqref{eq:bayes:bath},
we need specify, in addition to all of the details of the map $H$, the observation noise covariance $\Sigma$
and the prior distribution $\mu_0$ on $b$.

Our goal will be to
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)]  \emph{Lay down a physically sound, methodically precise rational for the specification of the elements defining
\eqref{eq:bayes:bath}.}  Partially for computational reasons we will require that $\mu_0$ be Gaussian but as we lay out below in \cref{sec:prior}
this is a quite flexible framework to incorporate existing estimates of the bottom Bathymetry (or to remain relatively neutral at the outset).
\item[(ii)] \emph{Formulate a numerical method to resolve (e.g. sample) the posterior $\mu$.}  Assuming a Gaussian prior we describe 
 several tractable `discretization independent' or `prior preconditioned' markov chain monte carlo (MCMC) methods which we may use to accomplish this task.
 See \cref{sec:MCMC}.
\item[(iii)] \emph{Systematically resolve \eqref{eq:bayes:bath} to obtain not just point estimates of $b$ but a complete
uncertainty quantification for this estimation problem.}  This will include resolution of credible sets, posterior covariances,
`push forwards' to other relevant observable quantities, sensitivity of the posterior to data and other model (hyper)parameters.
See \cref{sec:Bayes:Model:Eval}.
\item[(iii)] Place $\mu$ within a mathematically rigorous framework by establishing continuous dependence of $\mu$ on hyper parameters and data.  This mostly 
involves studying the forward map $H$.
\end{itemize}
